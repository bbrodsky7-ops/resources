Product Rule

\( a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n} \)
Intuition: An exponent is just a count of how many times you multiply by the base. So \( a^3 \cdot a^2 \) means three copies times two copies — five copies total: \( a^5 \).
ExpressionExpandedSimplified
\( x^3 \cdot x^4 \) \( (x \cdot x \cdot x)(x \cdot x \cdot x \cdot x) \) \( x^7 \)
\( 2^3 \cdot 2^5 \) \( 8 \cdot 32 \) \( 2^8 = 256 \)
\( y^{-2} \cdot y^6 \) add exponents: \(-2+6\) \( y^4 \)

Quotient Rule

\( \dfrac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n} \quad (a \neq 0) \)
Intuition: Division cancels factors. \( \dfrac{x^5}{x^2} = \dfrac{x \cdot x \cdot x \cdot \cancel{x} \cdot \cancel{x}}{\cancel{x} \cdot \cancel{x}} = x^3 \). You subtract because you're counting what's left after cancellation.
ExpressionStepResult
\( \dfrac{y^8}{y^3} \)\( 8-3 \)\( y^5 \)
\( \dfrac{3^6}{3^6} \)\( 6-6 \)\( 3^0 = 1 \)
\( \dfrac{x^2}{x^5} \)\( 2-5 \)\( x^{-3} \)
Exercise — Fill in the Blank
\( \dfrac{w^9}{w^4} = w^{\,?\,} \)
\( \dfrac{t^3}{t^7} = t^{\,?\,} \)   (negative answer ok)
Exercise — Step-by-Step
Simplify: \( \dfrac{6x^5 y^3}{2x^2 y^7} \)
  1. Divide the coefficients.
    \( \dfrac{6}{2} = 3 \)
  2. Apply the quotient rule to \( x \).
    \( x^{5-2} = x^3 \)
  3. Apply the quotient rule to \( y \).
    \( y^{3-7} = y^{-4} \)
  4. Write the final simplified answer.
    \( \dfrac{3x^3}{y^4} \)

Power Rule

\( (a^m)^n = a^{m \cdot n} \qquad (ab)^n = a^n b^n \qquad \left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^n = \dfrac{a^n}{b^n} \)
Intuition: \( (x^3)^4 \) means you repeat the group \(x^3\) four times: \( x^3 \cdot x^3 \cdot x^3 \cdot x^3 \). Product rule says add the exponents four times — which is the same as multiplying: \( x^{3 \times 4} = x^{12} \).
ExpressionResult
\( (k^4)^3 \)\( k^{12} \)
\( (2x^2)^3 \)\( 8x^6 \)
\( \left(\dfrac{p}{q^2}\right)^4 \)\( \dfrac{p^4}{q^8} \)

Zero Exponent

\( a^0 = 1 \quad (a \neq 0) \)
Intuition via quotient rule: Any number divided by itself is 1. Using the quotient rule: \( \dfrac{a^n}{a^n} = a^{n-n} = a^0 \). But we also know \( \dfrac{a^n}{a^n} = 1 \). So \( a^0 = 1 \).
Exercise — Multiple Choice
Evaluate: \( 7^0 \)
Which of the following equals 1?
Simplify: \( (xyz)^0 \) where \( x, y, z \neq 0 \)

Negative Exponents

\( a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n} \qquad \dfrac{1}{a^{-n}} = a^n \quad (a \neq 0) \)
Intuition: The quotient rule gives us \( \dfrac{a^2}{a^5} = a^{-3} \). But expanding shows \( \dfrac{a \cdot a}{a \cdot a \cdot a \cdot a \cdot a} = \dfrac{1}{a^3} \). Negative exponents mean reciprocal — they live in the denominator.
Negative formPositive form
\( x^{-3} \)\( \dfrac{1}{x^3} \)
\( \dfrac{1}{y^{-4}} \)\( y^4 \)
\( 2^{-3} \)\( \dfrac{1}{8} \)
\( \dfrac{a^{-2}}{b^{-5}} \)\( \dfrac{b^5}{a^2} \)
Exercise — Step-by-Step
Rewrite \( \dfrac{x^{-3} y^2}{z^{-1}} \) with only positive exponents.
  1. Move \( x^{-3} \) to the denominator.
    \( \dfrac{y^2}{x^3 \cdot z^{-1}} \)
  2. Move \( z^{-1} \) from denominator to numerator.
    \( \dfrac{y^2 \cdot z}{x^3} \)
  3. Write the final answer.
    \( \dfrac{y^2 z}{x^3} \)
Exercise — Fill in the Blank
\( 4^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{?} \)

Fractional Exponents

\( a^{1/n} = \sqrt[n]{a} \qquad a^{m/n} = \sqrt[n]{a^m} = \left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m \)
Intuition: If \( (a^{1/2})^2 = a^1 = a \), then \( a^{1/2} \) must be the number that, when squared, gives \(a\). That's exactly \( \sqrt{a} \). The denominator of the fractional exponent is the index of the radical.
Fractional formRadical formValue
\( 9^{1/2} \)\( \sqrt{9} \)\( 3 \)
\( 8^{1/3} \)\( \sqrt[3]{8} \)\( 2 \)
\( 16^{3/4} \)\( \left(\sqrt[4]{16}\right)^3 \)\( 8 \)
\( x^{2/5} \)\( \sqrt[5]{x^2} \)—

Multi-Step Practice Problems

Each problem requires at least two exponent properties. Work through them, use the hints if needed.

Problem 1 Easy
\( x^3 \cdot x^{-5} \cdot x^4 \)
Apply the product rule: add all three exponents together.
  1. Product rule: \( 3 + (-5) + 4 = 2 \)
  2. Answer: \( x^2 \)
Problem 2 Easy
\( \dfrac{(2a^3)^2}{a^4} \)
Apply the power rule to the numerator first, then the quotient rule.
  1. Power rule on numerator: \( (2a^3)^2 = 4a^6 \)
  2. Quotient rule: \( \dfrac{4a^6}{a^4} = 4a^{6-4} = 4a^2 \)
Problem 3 Medium
\( \dfrac{x^{-2} y^3}{x^4 y^{-1}} \)   (positive exponents only)
Apply the quotient rule to each variable separately, then rewrite negatives as reciprocals.
  1. Quotient on \(x\): \( x^{-2-4} = x^{-6} \)
  2. Quotient on \(y\): \( y^{3-(-1)} = y^4 \)
  3. Negative exponent: \( x^{-6} = \dfrac{1}{x^6} \)
  4. Answer: \( \dfrac{y^4}{x^6} \)
Problem 4 Medium
\( \left(\dfrac{a^2 b^{-1}}{a^{-3}}\right)^2 \)
Simplify inside the parentheses first using the quotient rule, then apply the power rule.
  1. Quotient on \(a\) inside: \( a^{2-(-3)} = a^5 \), so inside = \( a^5 b^{-1} \)
  2. Power rule: \( (a^5 b^{-1})^2 = a^{10} b^{-2} \)
  3. Answer: \( \dfrac{a^{10}}{b^2} \)
Problem 5 Medium
\( \dfrac{(3x^2 y)^3}{9x^4 y^0} \)
Remember \( y^0 = 1 \). Expand the numerator using the power rule first.
  1. Power rule: \( (3x^2 y)^3 = 27 x^6 y^3 \)
  2. Zero exponent: \( y^0 = 1 \), so denominator \( = 9x^4 \)
  3. Divide coefficients: \( \dfrac{27}{9} = 3 \)
  4. Quotient rule on \(x\): \( x^{6-4} = x^2 \)
  5. Answer: \( 3x^2 y^3 \)
Problem 6 Medium
\( \left(16x^8\right)^{1/2} \)
Distribute the \( \frac{1}{2} \) exponent to each factor inside using the power rule for products.
  1. Distribute: \( 16^{1/2} \cdot (x^8)^{1/2} \)
  2. \( 16^{1/2} = \sqrt{16} = 4 \)
  3. \( (x^8)^{1/2} = x^{8 \cdot \frac{1}{2}} = x^4 \)
  4. Answer: \( 4x^4 \)
Problem 7 Hard
\( \dfrac{(2x^{-1} y^3)^2 \cdot x^5}{4 y^{-2}} \)
Expand the squared factor first, then combine like bases across numerator and denominator.
  1. Power rule: \( (2x^{-1}y^3)^2 = 4x^{-2}y^6 \)
  2. Numerator: \( 4x^{-2}y^6 \cdot x^5 = 4x^{3}y^6 \)
  3. Full fraction: \( \dfrac{4x^3 y^6}{4y^{-2}} \)
  4. Coefficients cancel: \( \dfrac{4}{4} = 1 \)
  5. Quotient on \(y\): \( y^{6-(-2)} = y^8 \)
  6. Answer: \( x^3 y^8 \)
Problem 8 Hard
\( \left(\dfrac{x^{3/2} y^{-1}}{x^{-1/2} y^{1/2}}\right)^4 \)
Simplify the fraction inside first using the quotient rule (subtract exponents), then raise to the 4th power.
  1. Quotient on \(x\) inside: \( x^{3/2 - (-1/2)} = x^{2} \)
  2. Quotient on \(y\) inside: \( y^{-1 - 1/2} = y^{-3/2} \)
  3. Inside simplified: \( x^2 y^{-3/2} \)
  4. Power rule: \( (x^2 y^{-3/2})^4 = x^8 y^{-6} \)
  5. Negative exponent: \( y^{-6} = \dfrac{1}{y^6} \)
  6. Answer: \( \dfrac{x^8}{y^6} \)
Problem 9 Hard
\( \dfrac{(a^2 b^3)^{-2} \cdot a^5 b}{a^{-3} b^0} \)
Expand the \((\cdot)^{-2}\) first, then combine. Don't forget \( b^0 = 1 \).
  1. Power rule: \( (a^2 b^3)^{-2} = a^{-4} b^{-6} \)
  2. Numerator: \( a^{-4}b^{-6} \cdot a^5 b = a^1 b^{-5} \)
  3. Denominator: \( a^{-3} \cdot 1 = a^{-3} \) (since \( b^0=1 \))
  4. Quotient on \(a\): \( a^{1-(-3)} = a^4 \)
  5. Negative exponent: \( b^{-5} = \dfrac{1}{b^5} \)
  6. Answer: \( \dfrac{a^4}{b^5} \)
Problem 10 Hard
\( \left(\dfrac{8 x^{3/2}}{x^{-1/2}}\right)^{2/3} \)
Simplify the fraction inside first (quotient rule on \(x\)), then apply the fractional exponent to both the coefficient and the variable.
  1. Quotient on \(x\): \( x^{3/2-(-1/2)} = x^2 \)
  2. Inside: \( 8x^2 \)
  3. Apply \( (\cdot)^{2/3} \): \( 8^{2/3} \cdot (x^2)^{2/3} \)
  4. \( 8^{2/3} = (\sqrt[3]{8})^2 = 2^2 = 4 \)
  5. \( (x^2)^{2/3} = x^{4/3} \)
  6. Answer: \( 4x^{4/3} \)
